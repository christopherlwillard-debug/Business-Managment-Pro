Yes. I kept your original task intact but added two suggested improvements that I think are worth doing while you're already working in this area.

1. Database Transaction (Recommended)

Instead of recording a payment in several separate steps, wrap everything in a single database transaction.

That means:

Create the payment record.
Mark the selected advances as paid.
Update the subcontractor's paid total.
Update the payment status.
Commit everything together.

If something fails halfway through, nothing is saved, preventing inconsistent payroll data.

2. Advance Audit Trail (Recommended)

When an advance is deducted from a paycheck, store:

Which payment repaid it (payment_record_id)
When it was repaid (paid_at)

That lets you answer questions like:

"Which paycheck paid back this $50 fuel advance?"
"Has this advance actually been deducted yet?"
"When was it repaid?"

It's a much cleaner audit trail for payroll history.

That actually reinforces why I would keep the audit trail, but I'd tweak it slightly.

Since your advances are usually:

Cash you hand a subcontractor
Something you buy with your personal credit card (lunch, fuel, tools, etc.)

the system should treat them as business money you fronted, regardless of where it came from.

I would have each advance record include:

Funding Source
Business Cash
Personal Cash

Amount
Description
Date
Recorded by

Payment Record ID (which paycheck repaid it)

That gives you a complete history. For example:

Date	Type	Funding Source	Description	Amount	Status
Jul 10	Advance	Personal Card	Subway Lunch	$18.75	Repaid Jul 17
Jul 11	Advance	Cash	Fuel	$25.00	Outstanding
Jul 12	Advance	Personal Card	Drill Bits	$32.14	Repaid Jul 24

This also has another benefit for your business: if you're using your personal card to buy something for a subcontractor, you have documentation that the business owes you for that expense until it's effectively recovered through payroll. It creates a clear paper trail for bookkeeping and reimbursements.

Since you're building this as a long-term contractor management system, I'd also consider renaming "Cash Advance" to simply "Advance", with a funding source field. That way the feature accurately covers cash, personal credit cards, business credit cards, or any other way you front money for a subcontractor without being limited by the label "cash."

That's actually how I would model the system. I wouldn't tie advances to the payment method at all—they're separate concepts.

Advance (How you fronted the money)

This is how the subcontractor received money or value before payday.

Cash
Personal Credit Card
Business Credit Card
Personal Debit Card
Business Debit Card
Check
Other

Examples:

Bought lunch with your personal card.
Gave them $40 cash.
Paid for fuel on your business card.
Payment (How you paid their wages)

When payday comes, record how you actually paid them.

Payment methods might include:

Zelle
Cash App
Venmo
Cash
Check
ACH/Bank Transfer
Other

For example:

Advance Ledger

July 10 – Lunch – Personal Credit Card – $18.50
July 11 – Fuel – Cash – $25.00

Payroll

Gross Pay: $920.00
Less Advances: $43.50
Net Pay: $876.50
Paid via: Zelle
Payment Reference (optional): Zelle confirmation or transaction ID

This separation is important because:

You know how you fronted the advance.
You know how the subcontractor was ultimately paid.
Your bookkeeping stays accurate.
If you ever issue 1099s or need to review payroll, you have a complete history.
