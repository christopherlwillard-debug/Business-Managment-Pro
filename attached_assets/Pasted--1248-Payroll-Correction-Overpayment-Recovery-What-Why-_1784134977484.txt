#1248 - Payroll Correction: Overpayment Recovery

What & Why

Occasionally a subcontractor may be paid more than they actually earned because the payroll calculations were incorrect at the time of payment (for example, travel hours, profit split, or backend calculation bugs).

Instead of permanently inflating payroll, the system should recognize the difference as an overpayment credit that can be recovered from future payments.

This is not an advance against wages or a loan. It is simply correcting a previous payroll error.

Done Looks Like
If a subcontractor is paid more than their final calculated earnings, the payment status changes to Overpaid.
The overpaid amount is stored as a Payroll Correction Credit.
The job is still considered fully settled.
Future payroll sheets display:

Payroll Correction Available: $125.00

This subcontractor was previously overpaid due to a payroll calculation correction.

A one-click Apply Correction button deducts the credit from today's payment.
The owner can edit the amount if necessary.
The payment record shows:
Gross earnings
Payroll correction applied
Cash paid today
Once fully recovered, the correction is marked as resolved.
Out of Scope
Employee loans
Wage advances
Multi-business credits
Manual credit creation
Partial recovery logic (entire balance applied by default)
Backend Logic

If

amountPaid > amountOwed

then

paymentStatus = "overpaid"

correctionCredit = amountPaid - amountOwed

This credit remains attached to the subcontractor until recovered.

Why I like this approach better

Calling it an "Overpayment Credit" can make it sound like you're intentionally carrying balances between jobs or extending credit to subcontractors. In your case, that's not what's happening.

This feature exists because the software was wrong, not because of a business practice.

Using language like "Payroll Correction", "Payroll Adjustment", or "Overpayment Recovery" makes it clear that it's an accounting correction rather than a normal payroll workflow.

I would even label the notice in the UI as:

Payroll Correction Available

This subcontractor was previously overpaid due to a payroll calculation correction. Applying this adjustment reduces today's cash payment by the unrecovered amount.

That wording is much more accurate to the real-world scenario you described.
