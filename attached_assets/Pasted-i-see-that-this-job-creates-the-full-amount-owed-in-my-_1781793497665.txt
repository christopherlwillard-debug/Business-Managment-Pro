Correct. Based on how you've described wanting this system to work, there should only be one labor expense for the committed amount owed, not a new expense every time you make a payment.

Using your example:

Lot Model

Dewayne worked:

40 hrs × $25 = $1,000

The moment those hours are approved, the system should create:

Expense
Category: Subcontractor
Amount: $1,000
Job: Lot Model
Status: Unpaid

That's exactly what I'm seeing in your Expenses screen.

Then you pay him $200

The system should do:

Amount Owed: $1,000
Amount Paid: $200
Balance: $800
Status: Partial

But it should NOT create:

New Expense: $200

because then your books would show:

Labor Expense = $1,200

when the job only cost you:

Labor Expense = $1,000

which would overstate expenses and understate profit.

Think of it this way

The Expense record answers:

"What did this job cost me?"

The Payment record answers:

"How much of that cost have I paid so far?"

Those are different things.

Cost = $1,000
Paid = $200
Balance = $800

The cost never changes.

Only the payment ledger changes.

If the app is currently creating a new expense every time you click Record Payment, I would consider that a bug because you're mixing:

Expense Accounting

with

Cash Movement

The expense already exists when the labor is committed. Payments should only update the payment ledger and outstanding balance, not create additional labor expenses. That's the cleanest setup for your P&L, taxes, and year-end reporting.
