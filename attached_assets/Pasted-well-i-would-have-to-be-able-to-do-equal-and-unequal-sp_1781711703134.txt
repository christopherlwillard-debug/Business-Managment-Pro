That's exactly why I wouldn't build the system around "50/50", "33/33/33", or "equal split" at all.

I'd build it around job allocation percentages.

For every job, after:

Revenue
- Materials
- Expenses
- Hourly Labor
- Growth Reserve
= Split Pool

You assign percentages to the people participating in the split pool.

Example Today

You and one subcontractor:

Chris = 50%
Dewayne = 50%

Split Pool = $2,000

Chris = $1,000
Dewayne = $1,000
Example Tomorrow

Three people:

Chris = 34%
Dewayne = 33%
Worker 3 = 33%

Split Pool = $2,000

Chris = $680
Dewayne = $660
Worker 3 = $660
Example Future S-Corp

You want to keep more because you're handling the company:

Chris = 50%
Dewayne = 25%
Worker 3 = 25%

Split Pool = $2,000

Chris = $1,000
Dewayne = $500
Worker 3 = $500
Example Multi-Member LLC

If you eventually make someone a member:

Chris = 60%
Partner = 40%

or

Chris = 51%
Partner = 49%

Whatever your operating agreement requires.

One thing to be careful about:

Ownership percentage and job allocation percentage are not necessarily the same thing.

For example:

You could own 100% of the LLC.
Dewayne owns 0%.
Yet on a specific job you decide to split the job pool 50/50.

That's just a subcontractor payment arrangement, not ownership.

Likewise, if you become an S-Corp, you do not automatically have to receive 51% of every job. The legal requirements are more about stock ownership, wages, and distributions than individual job splits.

So for the app, I'd tell Replit:

Do not hardcode equal splits. Create a Job Allocation Percentage field for every worker assigned to the split pool. The percentages must total 100%. The system calculates each participant's share from the Split Pool based on their assigned percentage. This allows equal splits, unequal splits, S-Corp compensation structures, and future multi-member LLC arrangements without changing the database design.

That's flexible enough that you'll probably never need to redesign it later. You can run 50/50 today, 33/33/34 next month, and 60/20/20 five years from now using the same system.

That can be perfectly legitimate if they truly meet the independent contractor tests.

The bigger issue isn't LLC vs S-Corp. It's whether the workers are legally classified as contractors or employees.

Generally, the more they:

Work only for you
Use your tools
Follow your schedule
Work under your direct supervision
Perform the core service your company sells

the more likely regulators could view them as employees rather than independent contractors.

For your app, I'd future-proof it by having:

Worker Types
Owner
Employee
Subcontractor

Then separate that from:

Job Compensation Types
Hourly
Fixed Amount
Percentage Allocation
Equal Split

That way:

Dewayne

Worker Type: Subcontractor
Job A Compensation: Hourly
Job B Compensation: 40% Allocation

Worker #2

Worker Type: Employee
Job A Compensation: Hourly

The compensation method and worker classification stay separate.

For Willard's GC right now, I would build the app assuming:

You = Owner
Others = Subcontractors
Compensation assigned per job
Percentages must total 100% for everyone participating in the split pool

That structure will work whether you stay a single-member LLC, elect S-Corp taxation, or eventually hire employees. The database won't need to be redesigned later.
