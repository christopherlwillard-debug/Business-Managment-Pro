I actually think that’s the right direction, but after everything we’ve designed over the last month, I’d make one small change.

I wouldn’t call it “Work Category.”

I would call it Workflow.

That may sound like a small wording change, but it changes how the entire app is designed.

Instead of asking:

What type of job is this?

I’d ask:

Which workflow best fits this job?

Then give the user these options:

Manufactured Home Production

(Your primary business)

Used for:

* Trim outs
* Plumbing hookups
* Flooring
* Marriage line
* Underpinning
* Dealer work
* Factory work

This workflow unlocks:

* Builder & Model
* Smart Pricing
* Checklists
* Labor Summary
* Teach the Knowledge Base
* Factory Defects
* Missing Materials
* Historical Analytics

⸻

Manufactured Home Service

Used for:

* Warranty work
* Service calls
* Dealer punch lists
* Callbacks
* Repairs

This workflow includes:

* Builder & Model (optional but recommended)
* Warranty Tracking
* Service Notes
* Parts Used
* Photos

It does not feed the Knowledge Base because it isn’t representative of production labor.

⸻

General Contracting

Used for:

* Decks
* Kitchens
* Bathrooms
* Windows
* Doors
* Drywall
* Roofing
* Siding
* Remodeling
* Repairs

This workflow hides all the manufactured home fields because they aren’t relevant.

⸻

Why I think “Workflow” is the better name

A category is just a label.

A workflow changes how the app behaves.

That’s exactly what you’re trying to accomplish.

You’re not just categorizing jobs.

You’re telling the application:

“Build this job using the Manufactured Home Production workflow.”

or

“Build this using the Residential workflow.”

That gives the app permission to show different screens, different fields, different automation, different reporting, and different analytics.

⸻

My one addition

Since manufactured homes are your core business, I’d actually make the workflow selection the very first screen when creating a job.
