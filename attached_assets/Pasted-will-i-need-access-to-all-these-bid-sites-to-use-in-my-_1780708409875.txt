will i need access to all these bid sites to use in my app?

Not necessarily, but it depends on where the data comes from and what the site's terms allow.

There are generally 3 ways to get bid data:

1. Public APIs (Best Option)

Some procurement systems provide APIs or data feeds.

Examples include:

SAM.gov APIs
Various state open-data portals

Benefits:

More reliable
Less likely to break
Easier to scale
Generally allowed by the provider
2. RSS Feeds / Public Downloads

Many agencies publish:

RSS feeds
CSV exports
XML feeds
Public procurement notices

These are often easier and safer to integrate than scraping web pages.

3. Web Scraping

This is what many people start with.

The problem:

Website layouts change
Captchas get added
Login requirements appear
Terms of service may restrict automated collection

If you're building a commercial product, relying entirely on scraping can become a maintenance nightmare.

What I'd Do

Build your app with a layered approach:

Core Sources

Use official/public sources first:

SAM.gov
State procurement portals that provide feeds
County and municipal portals with public listings
Enhancement Layer

Use AI to:

Summarize opportunities
Score opportunities
Match NAICS codes
Generate capability statements

This is where your real value is.

Most users don't pay for raw bid listings. They pay because the software saves them time and helps them win.

For Your Business Specifically

Since you're in:

Mobile home finishing
Plumbing hookups
Water/sewer connections
Underpinning
Siding

You could get a lot of value from a narrower focus:

Instead of trying to ingest every bid in America, track:

Utility work
Plumbing
Construction
Site work
Manufactured housing
Renovation projects

Then build AI around finding the opportunities that fit your company.

A Bigger Opportunity

Since you already have:

Plaid integration
Square integration
CRM/workflow ideas

You could make the app do something most bid platforms don't:

Bid → Estimate → Contract → Invoice → Payment

Example:

Bid opportunity appears.
AI creates a preliminary estimate.
You convert it to a proposal.
Customer signs.
Square invoice is generated.
Project is tracked in the app.

That workflow is much harder for competitors to copy than simply aggregating bid notices.

So no, you do not need paid access to every bid site to get started. I'd start with official/public data sources and focus your development effort on the AI analysis and business workflow tools that actually help you win and manage jobs.
