Subcontractor Portal UX Improvements (V2)

Goal

Make the subcontractor portal feel like a premium mobile app rather than accounting software. Every page should answer three simple questions:

* What do I need to do next?
* How much have I made?
* How can I save money on taxes?

The overall experience should be clean, modern, actionable, and extremely easy for non-technical users.

⸻

Home Dashboard

Replace passive information with actionable cards.

Instead of simply displaying “W-9 Not Completed,” create an Action Required card that explains why the W-9 is important, estimates it takes about two minutes to complete, and includes a prominent “Complete W-9” button.

Once the W-9 is complete, replace the warning with a green success card stating that there are no outstanding tasks.

⸻

Dashboard Statistics

Keep the four statistic cards but make them more useful.

For YTD Payments, display the total amount and the number of payments received this year.

For Business Miles, display total miles and the estimated mileage deduction.

For Expenses, display the total expenses logged and the number of expense entries.

For Assigned Jobs, display total assigned jobs and active jobs.

This gives users context instead of only numbers.

⸻

Tax Readiness

Add a Tax Readiness section showing the user’s progress toward being ready for tax season.

Examples include:

* Income Recorded
* Mileage Logged
* W-9 Completed
* Expenses Logged
* Home Office Completed

Display an overall completion percentage with a progress bar.

⸻

Profile Completion

Add a profile completion progress bar near the top of the dashboard.

Show remaining tasks such as:

* Complete W-9
* Add first expense
* Complete Home Office deduction
* Verify mailing address

Users enjoy seeing measurable progress.

⸻

Empty States

Replace generic “No data” messages with helpful guidance.

Instead of saying “No expenses yet,” explain that tracking expenses can reduce taxable income and provide buttons to scan a receipt or add an expense.

Do the same for mileage by encouraging users to log business miles and explaining the potential tax benefit.

⸻

Expense Entry

Reorder the expense form to better match how users naturally enter expenses.

Recommended order:

Receipt

Description

Amount

Category

Job

Vendor

Date

Notes

Save Expense

Most users begin with a receipt, not by typing a description.

⸻

Quick Actions

Reduce the height of the Quick Action cards by approximately 25%.

Allow more actions to fit on screen without scrolling while maintaining clear descriptions.

⸻

Income Dashboard

Reduce the height of the large green income summary card.

Below it, add useful statistics such as:

* Number of payments received
* Average payment amount
* Largest payment
* Most recent payment

This provides valuable insights without adding clutter.

⸻

Payment History

Add icons based on payment type.

Examples:

Allocation Pay

Hourly Pay

Bonus

Advance

Reimbursement

Icons make the payment list much easier to scan.

⸻

Home Office Calculator

Convert the Home Office Calculator into a multi-step wizard.

Step 1:

Enter home square footage and office square footage.

Step 2:

Enter annual home expenses.

Step 3:

Display estimated deduction and estimated tax savings.

Breaking the calculator into steps makes it much less intimidating.

⸻

Estimated Tax Savings

Add a dashboard widget showing estimated tax savings.

As users log mileage, expenses, and home office information, display estimated deductions and an approximate tax savings estimate with a disclaimer that actual savings depend on their tax situation.

This encourages users to continue tracking expenses.

⸻

Tax Reports

Add a Tax Readiness Summary showing completed and incomplete tax items.

Examples include income, mileage, expenses, W-9, and home office deduction.

Display an overall readiness percentage so users know whether they are prepared for tax season.

⸻

Personalized Greeting

Replace the static “Welcome back” message with time-based greetings.

Examples:

Good morning, James.

Good afternoon, James.

Good evening, James.

This small detail makes the portal feel more polished and personal.

⸻

Color Consistency

Use a consistent color system throughout the portal.

Green for income and money received.

Blue for jobs.

Purple for tax calculations and deductions.

Orange for actions and warnings.

Red only for errors or overdue items.

Avoid using red for informational values.

⸻

Bottom Navigation

Add Expenses as a permanent navigation item.

Recommended navigation:

Home

Jobs

Income

Expenses

More

Expenses are one of the most frequently used features and should always be one tap away.

⸻

Take Home Summary

Add a simple earnings summary showing:

Income

Business Expenses

Mileage Deduction

Estimated Net Income

Most subcontractors care about how much money they actually made, so present this information using simple language instead of tax terminology.

⸻

Celebrate Milestones

Provide positive feedback when users complete important tasks.

Examples include:

W-9 completed.

First expense logged.

500 business miles reached.

Tax profile complete.

Small celebrations make the portal feel more engaging and rewarding.

⸻

Overall Design Philosophy

The subcontractor portal should feel like a personal business assistant rather than accounting software.

Every screen should clearly answer:

* What should I do next?
* How much have I earned?
* How can I save money on taxes?

Prioritize simplicity, actionable guidance, visual hierarchy, mobile-first design, and subtle positive feedback. The end result should feel premium, approachable, and comparable to modern apps like Stripe, QuickBooks Self-Employed, and Rocket Money while remaining tailored specifically to subcontractors.
