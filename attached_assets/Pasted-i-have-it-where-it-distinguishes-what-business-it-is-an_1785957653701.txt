I think you're at the point where BuildPro shouldn't just "track transactions"—it should become an accounting engine that changes its behavior based on the selected business entity.

Right now it sounds like it's using one set of accounting rules for every business, and that's the problem.

I'd redesign it like this:

Business Settings
↓
Business Tax Profile
↓
Accounting Engine
↓
Financial Reports

Each business type gets its own accounting rules.

Example Rules
Sole Proprietorship / Single-Member LLC
Owner Draw = Equity Withdrawal
NOT a business expense
Does NOT reduce Net Profit
Taxable profit belongs to the owner whether money is withdrawn or not.

I would also build an Accounting Rules Engine.

Instead of hardcoding calculations everywhere, create a single configuration for each entity type:

BusinessEntity {
    ownerDrawTreatment
    payrollTreatment
    distributionTreatment
    taxComputation
    equityRules
    depreciationRules
    retirementRules
    quarterlyTaxRules
}

Then every report—P&L, Balance Sheet, Job Profit, Owner Dashboard, Tax Reports—uses those rules. That way:

If someone changes from a Single-Member LLC to an S Corp, the reports automatically switch to the correct accounting treatment.
You avoid scattered if (entity === ...) logic throughout the app.
Adding future entity types becomes much easier.

I think this is one of the biggest opportunities for BuildPro. Most contractor software only tracks transactions. BuildPro can actually understand the contractor's business structure and present the numbers the way they should be reported for that entity. That would make the accounting side of the app much more valuable than simply acting as a ledger.
