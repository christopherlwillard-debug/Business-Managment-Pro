I think this is an excellent idea, but I'd make one significant architectural change.

Don't let the user choose "Tax Treatment."

The IRS doesn't let you arbitrarily choose it. It's determined by how the business is actually taxed (unless they've made an election). If you expose both fields as editable, you can create invalid combinations like:

Entity Type: S Corporation
Tax Treatment: Partnership

That shouldn't be possible.

well someone can switch from sole to s corp

Exactly—and that's why I would separate Legal Entity from Federal Tax Election.

A business can change how it's taxed over time, but the legal entity usually stays the same.

For example, your business today is:

Legal Entity: Single-Member LLC
Federal Tax Election: Sole Proprietorship (default/disregarded entity)

If you file Form 2553 and the IRS accepts it, it becomes:

Legal Entity: Single-Member LLC (unchanged)
Federal Tax Election: S Corporation (changed)

You didn't become a corporation—you remained an LLC that elected S corporation taxation.

I would also add an Effective Date

This is something many accounting apps overlook.

Tax Profile

Legal Entity:
Single-Member LLC

Federal Tax Election:
S Corporation

Effective Date:
01/01/2027

Then your accounting engine knows:

2026 reports → Sole Proprietorship rules
2027 reports → S Corporation rules

That becomes important because businesses often elect S corporation status effective January 1 of a tax year. Historical reports should continue using the rules that were in effect at that time.

I think adding an effective date now will save you from a much more difficult migration later and make your accounting reports historically accurate.
