The full August 20 forensic audit confirms that cash-basis revenue timing is inconsistent, but it also identified related source-of-truth problems involving business ownership, subcontractor payments, mileage predicates, IRS rates, owner activity, and reversal semantics.

Do NOT immediately refactor reports merely to make totals agree.

PHASE 1 — READ-ONLY VERIFICATION

Before changing code, independently identify the authoritative source records for:

1. Customer cash receipts
2. Invoice issue dates
3. Effective payment dates
4. Partial payments
5. Business ownership
6. Subcontractor payments
7. Owner activity
8. Mileage
9. Mileage IRS rates
10. Reversals

For each report surface currently called cash-basis, document exactly which source tables, dates, business filters, and predicates it uses.

The authoritative rule for cash-basis revenue should be:

ACTUAL CASH RECEIVED DURING THE REPORTING PERIOD.

Invoice issue date must NOT determine the tax/reporting year when the payment was received in another year.

Do not modify historical payment dates or invoice records.

PHASE 2 — BUSINESS OWNERSHIP

Do not automatically treat business_id/business_line NULL as belonging to the selected business.

Determine and document an explicit policy for:
- Willard's General Contracting
- Willard's Asphalt Sealing
- Unassigned records
- All-business reporting

A record must never appear in two businesses simply because its business ownership is NULL.

Do not backfill ownership without a separate approved forensic/data-migration task.

PHASE 3 — MILEAGE

Use one canonical mileage predicate everywhere.

Owner standard-mileage deduction must:
- exclude subcontractor mileage
- include only appropriate owner/business mileage
- use the correct trip date
- use the correct IRS rate for that date
- use the same predicate for miles, dollars, counts, warnings and Schedule C

For 2026 specifically:
- Jan 1–Jun 30 = $0.725/mile
- Jul 1 onward = $0.760/mile

Do not use a single annual rate for 2026.

Do not silently recalculate or alter historical mileage records in this task.

PHASE 4 — SUBCONTRACTOR PAYMENTS

Expenses, Accounting, P&L, Schedule C and subcontractor balances must distinguish:

EARNED / ALLOCATED COMPENSATION
vs.
CASH PAID
vs.
ADVANCES
vs.
REMAINING BALANCE

Example:
James earns $600.
James receives a $60 advance.
Remaining owed = $540.
Contract Labor = $600.
Total cash paid when settled = $600.

The $60 advance must not create another $60 expense.

Every James sub_payment_record must be traceable to:
- earned amount
- advances
- subsequent payments
- total cash paid
- remaining balance
- related job
- related expense row, if any

Do not reclassify James's tax status in this task.

PHASE 5 — OWNER ACTIVITY

Reconcile owner_capital_entries against owner-related expense records.

Determine which source is authoritative for:
- owner draw
- owner compensation
- owner withdrawal
- business contribution

Owner activity must remain excluded from deductible business expenses while remaining visible in Owner Pay/cash-flow reporting.

Do not create duplicate historical transactions.

PHASE 6 — REVERSALS

Verify the actual semantics of reversal records.

Determine whether a reversal:
- replaces the original
- offsets the original
- marks the original as reversed
- or represents a separate economic event

Do not assume `isReversal = true` alone is sufficient.

Add a controlled fixture proving the accounting result.

ONLY AFTER PHASES 1–6 ARE VERIFIED:

Create one shared cash-revenue helper.

It must power:
- P&L
- Schedule C
- Dashboard
- accounting exports
- any other report claiming cash-basis revenue

It must use effective payment dates and correctly handle:
- prior-year invoice paid in current year
- current-year invoice paid next year
- partial payments

- refunds/reversals
- business ownership
- unassigned records
