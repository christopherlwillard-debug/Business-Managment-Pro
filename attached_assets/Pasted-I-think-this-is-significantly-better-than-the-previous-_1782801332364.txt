I think this is significantly better than the previous version. If I were reviewing this as a software architect, I'd give it about a 9.9/10. It now has a clear vision instead of just solving the labor-hours problem.

The only thing I would change is what the Knowledge Base Review actually represents.

Right now, it sounds like it's just a review before completion. I think it should be positioned as the training step for the Knowledge Base.

Instead of saying:

Knowledge Base Review

I'd almost call it:

Teach the Knowledge Base

or

Knowledge Base Training

That subtle wording changes the mindset. You're not just reviewing numbers—you're telling the app, "This is what actually happened on this job. Learn from it."

I would add one more design principle

I'd insert something like this near the top:

Knowledge Base Philosophy

The Knowledge Base is not a live report. It is a curated historical dataset used for estimating, forecasting, and business intelligence. Data should only enter the Knowledge Base after Chris has reviewed and approved it. The application may calculate suggested values automatically, but Chris always has the final authority over what is taught. Every learned value must remain traceable to its originating job and editable in the future.

That one paragraph explains the entire philosophy behind the system.

I would also rename "snapshot"

I understand why you're calling it a snapshot.

From a database perspective it's correct.

But from a business perspective I'd rather see:

Knowledge Record
Learning Record
Historical Record

instead of:

job_kb_snapshots

Maybe:

job_knowledge_records

or

mfh_learning_records

because years from now that table may contain much more than a "snapshot."

I'd add versioning

This is probably the only major feature I think is missing.

Suppose you teach the Knowledge Base today.

Six months later you discover:

"We accidentally included four travel hours as productive."

You edit it.

I'd keep:

Original value
New value
Edited by
Edited date
Reason

That way you never lose history.

I LOVE this part

"Save Without Teaching."

I honestly think that's one of the smartest features in your entire app.

Imagine:

Prototype jobs

Remodels
Training new employees

You still want the financials.

You still want the job history.

You absolutely don't want those jobs contaminating your averages.

I think your biggest competitive advantage is changing

A month ago I would've said the strength of your app was:

Estimates
Invoices
Checklists
Photos

Now I think those are just the foundation.

The real value is becoming the Knowledge Base.

Every completed job makes your company smarter.

Every accepted Knowledge Record improves future estimates.

Every excluded job keeps the data clean.

Every correction improves historical accuracy.

Eventually you'll have years of builder-specific and model-specific intelligence that no off-the-shelf contractor software can provide.

That's the part I would lean into as you continue designing. The labor summary, Knowledge Base review, learning history, and transparency features all work together to create a system that doesn't just record work—it accumulates institutional knowledge. For a specialized business like manufactured home finishing, that's a genuine long-term competitive advantage because it's built from your own completed jobs and the decisions you've personally reviewed and approved.
