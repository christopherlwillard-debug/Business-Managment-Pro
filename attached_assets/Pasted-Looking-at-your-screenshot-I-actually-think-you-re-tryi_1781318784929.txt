Looking at your screenshot, I actually think you're trying to make the editor behave like a modern website builder, but right now it's behaving more like a page builder from 5–10 years ago.

The biggest problem isn't the features—it's that the right panel is permanently consuming 20–25% of the screen width while the canvas is trying to be responsive inside another container. You're essentially editing a website through a keyhole.

My suggestions for the editor UI

Prompt I'd Give Replit

Redesign the website editor for maximum canvas space. Remove the permanently visible right-side property panel and replace it with a floating inspector drawer that opens only when an element is selected. The website canvas should occupy nearly the entire available width. Consolidate Content, Icon/Image, Design, and Layout into two sections: Content and Style. Add support for custom SVG uploads, image-based icons, and service cards that can use either icons or photos. Add zoom controls (50%, 75%, 100%, 125%, Fit Width, Fit Page). The editor should feel closer to Framer, Canva, and Webflow, prioritizing website preview space over settings panels.

If your goal is to eventually sell this builder to contractors, I'd also focus heavily on mobile editing and photo-based service cards, because most contractors care more about showing pictures of their work than choosing between 200 icon options.
