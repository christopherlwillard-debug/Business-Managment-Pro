The IRS offers two methods to calculate your home office deduction: the simplified option uses a flat rate of $5 per square foot of the office space (up to a maximum of 300 square feet, capping the deduction at $1,500). Alternatively, the regular method lets you deduct the exact percentage of your actual housing expenses used for business.The rules and calculations for these methods break down as follows:1. The Simplified OptionInstead of tracking individual receipts, you can use the standard IRS rate.The Rate: $5 per square footSize Limit: Maximum of 300 square feetMaximum Deduction: $1,500How it works: Multiply the square footage of your dedicated office space by $5. For example, a 200-square-foot office would yield a $1,000 deduction.2. The Regular Method (Actual Expenses)If your office space is large or you have high housing costs, you may want to use actual expenses.Calculation: You deduct the percentage of your home devoted to business use. For example, if your home is 1,500 square feet and your office takes up 150 square feet (10%), you can deduct 10% of your total allowable housing expenses.Deductible Costs: You can write off the business-use portion of your mortgage interest, real estate taxes, utilities, insurance, and repairs.Eligibility and Important NotesTo qualify to use either method, your home office must pass two strict tests:Regular and Exclusive Use: The space must be used only for business on a regular basis.Principal Place of Business: Your home must be the primary location of your trade or business, or a place where you regularly meet with clients or customers.Note: Under current tax laws, standard W-2 employees are ineligible to claim the home office deduction.
