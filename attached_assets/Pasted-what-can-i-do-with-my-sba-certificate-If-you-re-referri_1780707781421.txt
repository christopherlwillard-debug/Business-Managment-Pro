what can i do with my sba certificate 

If you're referring to your Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB) certification, it can open several doors, but it's important to understand what it does and doesn't do.

What You Can Do With It
1. Compete for Federal Set-Aside Contracts

Federal agencies can reserve certain contracts exclusively for SDVOSB-certified companies. This means you're competing against a much smaller pool of businesses instead of every contractor in the country.

Key agencies that frequently use veteran-owned contractors include:

Department of Veterans Affairs
Department of Defense
General Services Administration
Department of Homeland Security

For your mobile home finishing and hookup business, opportunities may include:

Plumbing work
Utility hookups
Site improvements
Renovations and repairs
Construction support services
Disaster recovery work
2. Register for Government Contracting Platforms

You should make sure your business is registered in:

SAM.gov
SBA Veteran Small Business Certification Program

Without an active SAM registration, agencies generally can't award federal contracts to you.

3. Market Yourself as a Certified SDVOSB

Add the certification to:

Your website
Email signature
Business cards
Vehicle graphics
Capability statements
Social media

For example:

SBA-Certified Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB)

This helps prime contractors looking to meet subcontracting goals find you.

4. Become a Subcontractor

You don't have to win federal contracts directly.

Large construction companies often need SDVOSB subcontractors to satisfy contract requirements. This can be one of the fastest ways to start generating government-related revenue.

5. Access Some State and Local Programs

Many states, cities, and utility authorities recognize veteran-owned businesses and may offer:

Bid preferences
Supplier diversity programs
Networking opportunities

You'll need to check the programs in the states where you operate.

6. Pursue GSA Schedule Opportunities

Eventually you may want to explore a contract through the General Services Administration that allows agencies to buy services from you more easily.

What It Does NOT Do

The certification does not:

Automatically award contracts
Provide grants
Guarantee funding
Generate customers by itself

You still need to market the business and pursue opportunities.

What I'd Do Next If I Were You

Given that you're running a mobile-home finishing and hookup company, I'd focus on:

Verify your SAM registration is active.
Create a one-page capability statement.
Add the SDVOSB certification to your website and email signature.
Start looking for subcontracting opportunities with larger construction and utility contractors.
Search for federal opportunities involving plumbing, utility connections, renovations, and site work.

If you want, I can help you build a professional SDVOSB capability statement specifically tailored to your mobile home finishing, plumbing hookups, water/sewer connections, underpinning, and siding services. That's often the first document contracting officers and prime contractors ask for.
