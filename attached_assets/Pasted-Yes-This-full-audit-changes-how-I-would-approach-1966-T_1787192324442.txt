Yes. This full audit changes how I would approach #1966.

The audit confirms that the cash-basis problem is real, but it also shows that #1966 is only one piece of a much larger accounting/source-of-truth problem.

What the audit actually confirmed

The important financial finding is:

P&L and Schedule C are using invoice issue date in places where cash-basis reporting should use the effective payment date.

That’s a legitimate defect and #1966 addresses it.

But there are several related findings that need to be handled together:

1. H1 — Cash-basis revenue timing
    * P&L/Schedule C use issue date.
    * Other accounting paths use effective payment date.
    * This can put revenue in the wrong tax year.
2. H2 — Unassigned business records
    * Schedule C can include business_line = NULL records in whichever business you’re viewing.
    * That’s dangerous for your Willard’s GC vs. Asphalt businesses.
3. H3 — Mileage predicates
    * Deduction and mileage-count logic aren’t using exactly the same filters.
4. H4 — IRS rates
    * There are multiple rate tables.
    * This is especially important given what you just found about 2026 being $0.725 Jan–Jun and $0.760 beginning July 1.
5. H5 — Subcontractor payments
    * Expenses combines the expense table and sub_payment_records.
    * Business filtering isn’t consistent between Expenses, Accounting and Schedule C.
6. H7 — Reversals
    * Needs verification so a reversal isn’t merely excluded while the original payment remains counted.
7. H6 — Owner activity
    * There are competing owner-draw/owner-capital paths that need reconciliation.
8. M1/M2 — Job profit
    * Job profit uses invoiced revenue while Accounting uses cash revenue.
    * There are multiple job-profit calculations.

So I would NOT tell Replit:

“Go ahead and fix #1966.”

I’d tell it:

“Implement #1966 only after proving the underlying source-of-truth and reconciliation rules.”

Because we’ve already seen what happens when we tell the app to make two numbers match without first proving the underlying records.
