This version is much safer than the earlier version.

The biggest improvement is that you're doing a dual-write transition first instead of immediately ripping payment data out of expenses. That's exactly how I would handle an accounting system.

There are only 3 things I'd still change:

1. Add job_subcontractor_snapshot

Right now you're storing:

job_id
subcontractor_id
amount
payment_date

But imagine this:

James is owed $617.50
You pay him $200
Later you edit Agreed Owed to $700
Later you pay another $500

Years later, you'll want to know:

What agreement existed when that payment was made?

I'd add:

agreed_owed_snapshot NUMERIC(10,2)

on the payment record.

That preserves historical context.

2. Add payment correction support now

You already know you're going to need it because you created Task #870.

Add:

is_reversal BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE
reverses_payment_id INTEGER NULL

Now if you accidentally enter:

$417.50

instead of

$400.00

you can create:

-$17.50

as a reversal entry.

Accountants love immutable ledgers.

3. Don't call it "1099 income"

Call it:

1099 Reportable Payments

or

Cash Payments This Year

throughout the code.

Reason:

The portal is showing payments.

The IRS cares about reportable payments.

Calling it "income" tends to blur:

Owed
Income
Expense
Payment

which are all different things.

What I like most about this version

You finally have three completely separate concepts:

Job Cost
expenses

Answers:

What did this job cost?

Payment History
sub_payment_records

Answers:

What did I actually pay James?

Outstanding Balance
job_subcontractors
amountOwed
amountPaid

Answers:

What do I still owe James?

That separation is what your app has been missing.

Before this, one table was trying to answer all three questions at once.

After #887 and #888 are finished, your job costing, sub payments, portal, 1099s, outstanding balances, closed jobs, and year-end reporting will all be operating from much cleaner accounting logic.

I would move forward with #887 first, then build this version of #888.
