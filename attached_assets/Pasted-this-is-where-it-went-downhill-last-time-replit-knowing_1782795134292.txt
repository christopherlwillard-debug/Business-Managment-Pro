I remember this, and I think this is exactly why the previous design became confusing.

Looking at your screenshot, Replit interpreted the fields like this:

Manufacturer: Clayton
Model: Southern

But that's not how you identify homes in the field.

For your workflow, it's more like:

Client: Clayton Homes Evansville (who hired you)
Builder: Southern (who built the home / what everyone calls it)
Model: Farmhouse (the floorplan/model name)

So for that example it should be:

Client: Clayton Homes Evansville
Builder: Southern
Model: Farmhouse
Construction: VOG
Year: 2022 (optional)

Not:

Manufacturer: Clayton ❌
Model: Southern ❌

The reason Replit did this is because it treated Clayton as the manufacturer of the home instead of the dealer/client. In your business, those are two completely different concepts.

I think the UI should actually be simplified

I'd even rename the section to remove any ambiguity.

Instead of:

Manufactured Home

Manufacturer
Model

I'd use:

Manufactured Home

Builder (Southern, Giles, Norris, etc.)

Model (Farmhouse, Magnolia, etc.)

Construction (auto)

Year (optional)

That's exactly how you and everyone else in your industry refer to homes.

I'd also make this explicit in your task

I would add a requirement like this:

Terminology

The Manufactured Home selector should use Builder and Model because this matches the terminology used in day-to-day operations.

The selected Client represents the dealer or customer that the job is being performed for and is independent of the Builder.

Example:

Client: Clayton Homes Evansville
Builder: Southern
Model: Farmhouse

That removes any room for interpretation.
