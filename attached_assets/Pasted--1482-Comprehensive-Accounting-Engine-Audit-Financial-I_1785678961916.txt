#1482 - Comprehensive Accounting Engine Audit & Financial Integrity Review

Design Principles

BuildPro must have one authoritative accounting engine.

Every financial number displayed anywhere in the application must originate from a single source of truth. No page should calculate its own totals independently if the same value already exists elsewhere.

Every financial transaction should be classified exactly once, affect the correct accounts exactly once, and never be double-counted.

This task is an audit first, not a feature task.

Do not redesign the UI until the accounting engine has been verified.

What & Why

As BuildPro has grown, accounting logic has been added across many different modules including Jobs, Invoices, Banking, Expenses, Payroll, Owner Pay, Owner Capital, Investments, Accounting & Tax, Reports, Dashboard, and Profit calculations.

Before adding more accounting features, perform a complete audit of every financial calculation in the application to ensure:

• No double counting
• No missing transactions
• No inconsistent formulas
• No duplicated business logic
• One source of truth for every financial value

The objective is to ensure every number shown anywhere in BuildPro can be traced back to a single calculation path.

Done looks like

Every financial calculation has been audited.

Every financial page has a documented source of truth.

Duplicate calculations have been eliminated.

No transaction can affect financial reports more than once unless intentionally designed.

Owner Capital, Owner Pay, Banking, Investments, Payroll, Expenses, Invoices, Profit & Loss, Dashboard, Tax Reports, and Equity all reconcile correctly.

Every displayed number can be traced through the application.

No page performs independent calculations that disagree with another page.

3. Double Counting Audit

Search for every situation where a transaction could be counted twice.

Examples:

Expense also reducing Job Profit twice

Invoice counted as revenue before payment

Owner Draw reducing equity multiple times

Capital Contribution also treated as revenue

Investment Transfer treated as Expense

Payroll affecting profit twice

Transfer between bank accounts counted as income

Owner Pay appearing on multiple pages

Job Payment affecting Dashboard and P&L separately

--------------------------------------------------

4. Owner Accounting Audit

Carefully review:

Owner Capital

Owner Contributions

Owner Withdrawals

Owner Draws

Owner Pay

Owner Equity

Retained Earnings

Business Profit

Determine whether these concepts are being mixed together.

Owner Pay should represent:

Money actually paid from the business to the owner.

Owner Capital should represent:

Money personally invested into the business.

Owner Equity should represent:

Capital plus retained business profit.

Owner Draws should never be duplicated across multiple pages unless intentionally referenced.

Verify that these concepts are separated correctly.

--------------------------------------------------

Deposits

Payments

9. Source of Truth Audit

Replace duplicate implementations with shared accounting services wherever practical.

Engineering Requirements

This task is primarily an audit.

Do not rewrite large portions of the accounting system unless an error is confirmed.

Reuse existing accounting services whenever possible.

If multiple implementations exist, consolidate them into one shared calculation rather than maintaining parallel logic.

Every financial calculation should have one authoritative implementation.

Deliverables

Provide a written audit report identifying:

• Duplicate calculations

• Double-counting risks

• Missing transactions

• Incorrect accounting treatment

• Inconsistent formulas

• Pages using outdated calculations

• Recommended consolidations

• High-risk financial bugs

Do not silently change financial logic.

Document every recommended correction before implementing major accounting changes.
