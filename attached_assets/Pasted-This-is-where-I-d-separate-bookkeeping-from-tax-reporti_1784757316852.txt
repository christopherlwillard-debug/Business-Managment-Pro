This is where I’d separate bookkeeping from tax reporting. Trying to calculate taxes from bank transactions alone will eventually create problems.

Here’s the approach I’d recommend:

During the year

Use the app to track:

* Contributions to the investment account.
* Withdrawals from the investment account.
* Current portfolio value (manual or synced).
* Dividend deposits that hit your bank account (if you want to track investment income).

This gives you a good picture of your business investments throughout the year.

At tax time

Don’t rely on Plaid for tax reporting.

Instead, import the official year-end tax documents from your brokerage, such as:

* Annual brokerage statement.
* Form 1099-DIV (dividends).
* Form 1099-B (sales of investments).
* Form 1099-INT (interest), if applicable.

Those are the documents your tax preparer (or tax software) will use because they contain the official, reportable amounts.

How I’d build it

Create a Tax Documents section under each investment account.

Allow users to upload:

* Year-end statements (PDF).
* 1099 forms (PDF).
* Monthly or quarterly statements (optional).

Then the app could:

* Store the documents by tax year.
* Let users retrieve them later.
* Potentially extract key numbers with AI in the future (optional).

Example

Business Brokerage

* 2026 Annual Statement.pdf
* 2026 1099-DIV.pdf
* 2026 1099-B.pdf

Then, when preparing taxes, everything is already organized in one place.

I would not try to replace the brokerage’s tax reporting

Even if Plaid provides transaction data, it generally isn’t a substitute for the brokerage’s official tax forms. Things like cost basis adjustments, wash sales, qualified dividends, and other tax-specific reporting are determined by the brokerage and reported on the 1099s.

My recommendation

I would have the app do two things:

1. Track investments throughout the year for management and reporting.
2. Store the official brokerage tax documents at year-end for tax preparation.

That gives you the best of both worlds: real-time visibility into your investments and accurate, official records when it’s time to file taxes. I think that’s a much more reliable long-term design than trying to have the app calculate every tax figure itself.
