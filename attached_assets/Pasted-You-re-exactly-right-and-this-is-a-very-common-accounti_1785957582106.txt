You're exactly right, and this is a very common accounting mistake in contractor software.

For a single-member LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship, your "owner pay" is not a business expense.

Here's why:

The business earns the profit.
That profit is taxable to you whether you leave the money in the business or transfer it to your personal account.
Moving money from the business account to yourself is an Owner's Draw, not payroll and not an expense.

# Financial Fix – Owner Draw vs Business Expense

For businesses with a Tax Profile of:

• Sole Proprietorship
• Single-Member LLC (Disregarded Entity)

Owner Draws must NOT be treated as business expenses.

Changes Required

1. Remove Owner Draws from the Job Expenses section and from all profit calculations.

2. Create a new "Owner Activity" section below Net Business Profit.

3. Record Owner Draws as equity withdrawals only.

4. Net Profit should always be calculated before any Owner Draws.

5. Add the following summary:

• Net Business Profit
• Growth Reserve
• Estimated Tax Reserve
• Distributable Profit
• Owner Draws Taken
• Remaining Available Owner Draw

6. Profit & Loss reports should never reduce Net Profit because of Owner Draws.

7. Continue tracking Owner Draw history for reporting purposes, but classify those transactions as equity withdrawals rather than expenses.

This change should automatically apply to Sole Proprietorships and Single-Member LLCs based on the Business Tax Profile. Future entity types (Partnerships, S Corps, C Corps) can continue using entity-specific owner compensation rules.

This also aligns perfectly with the Business Tax Profile system you've already been building. When the business type changes (for example, to an S corporation), the accounting treatment can change automatically without changing the rest of the job workflow.
