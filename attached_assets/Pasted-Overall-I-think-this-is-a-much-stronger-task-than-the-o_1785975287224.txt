Overall, I think this is a much stronger task than the original. It's focused on architecture instead of patching symptoms.

There are just three changes I'd make before giving it to Replit.

1. Don't assume there are exactly two engines

Right now it says:

"there are at least two separate OSRM geocoding pipelines..."

Instead say:

Do not assume how many routing implementations exist.

The first step is to audit the entire codebase and identify every implementation of:

- OSRM routing
- geocoding
- business distance
- client distance
- drive time
- distance caching

Only after the audit may consolidation begin.

That follows your Constitution better because you're requiring evidence before conclusions.

2. Build a Route Engine, not just a Distance Engine

I actually think BuildPro is going to need more than mileage.

Eventually you'll need:

miles
drive time
tolls
ferries
fuel estimate
routing failures
future traffic

So instead of

calculateDrivingRoute(...)

I'd define the engine like this:

RouteEngine.calculate({
    origin,
    destination,
    vehicleProfile,
})

returns

{
    miles,
    driveTimeMinutes,
    estimatedFuelGallons,
    estimatedFuelCost,
    routeStatus,
    provider,
    cached
}

Then:

General Contracting

Business → Job

Manufactured Housing

Business → Job

Client → Job

Future

Supplier → Job

Warehouse → Job

Storage Yard → Job

all call the same engine.

That'll save you rewriting it in six months.

3. Never calculate fuel outside the engine

This is the biggest thing I'd add.

Right now the task says

distance
÷ mpg
× fuel price

I wouldn't let every module do that.

I'd say:

The Route Engine owns every operational routing calculation.

Callers receive:

- miles
- drive time
- estimated fuel gallons
- estimated fuel cost

No module may calculate fuel independently.

Otherwise six months from now you'll find

Jobs

Mileage

Route Planner

Dashboard

Reports

all doing

distance / mpg * fuelPrice

slightly differently.

I would also add Rule 53
Rule 53 — One Engine Owns All Calculations

If an engine is responsible for a business capability, it owns every calculation related to that capability.

Examples

Distance Engine
• Miles
• Drive Time
• Fuel Gallons
• Fuel Cost
• Route Cache

Tax Engine
• Taxable Income
• Self Employment Tax
• Federal Tax
• Estimated Payments

Profit Engine
• Revenue
• Expenses
• Growth
• Payroll
• Split Pool
• Owner Pay

No caller may recreate part of an engine's calculations.
One thing I really like

This sentence:

"Two legitimate distance types exist (not duplicate engines)."

That captures your architecture perfectly.

You aren't saying:

"Only one distance."

You're saying:

"Many routes, one routing engine."

That's the distinction that will keep BuildPro from drifting into duplicated logic as it grows.
