What you’re describing is actually a very smart direction for a contractor app.
Most contractor apps only do:
estimates
invoices
CRM
But very few help contractors think through:
permits
inspections
sequencing
licensing
risk management
scope progression
That’s where you could build something genuinely useful.
Here’s how I’d structure it if I were rebuilding that section of your Replit app.

Core Idea
Instead of just:
“Create estimate”
You build:
“Project Intelligence Workflow”
Where the app acts like an experienced GC assistant.

Recommended System Structure
1. Job Intake Wizard
Start every project with structured questions.
Example:
What type of job?
remodel
roofing
electrical
windows
deck
addition
demolition
Residential or commercial?
Structural changes?
Utility changes?
New circuits?
Plumbing relocation?
Square footage?
Occupied property?
HOA?
County/city?
This becomes the foundation for automation.

2. Permit Trigger Engine
This is the goldmine feature.
Based on answers, your app flags:
likely permits required
likely inspections required
likely licensed trades required
if (job.electricalNewCircuits === true) {
  triggers.push({
    type: "permit",
    message: "Electrical permit likely required."
  });

  triggers.push({
    type: "license",
    message: "Licensed electrician may be required."
  });
}

You’re basically building:
“Contractor GPS”

3. Project Phase Workflow
This is HUGE for new contractors.
The app should automatically generate project phases.
Example:
Kitchen Remodel
Demo
Framing
Electrical rough-in
Plumbing rough-in
Inspection
Insulation
Drywall
Cabinets
Flooring
Trim
Final fixtures
Final inspection
Then allow:
status tracking
photos
notes
approvals

4. Risk Alerts
This is where you separate yourself from normal contractor apps.
Examples:
Alert
“Customer requested structural wall removal. Engineer review may be needed.”
Alert
“Window replacement on brick exterior may require lintel inspection.”
Alert
“Project exceeds homeowner permit thresholds in many jurisdictions.”
That’s EXTREMELY valuable.

5. Licensing Matrix
Build a database by:
state
county
trade
Example:
{
  "state": "KY",
  "trade": "Electrical",
  "permitRequired": true,
  "licenseRequired": true
}

Even if imperfect initially, users love guidance.

6. Scope-to-Trade Breakdown
This helps new GCs understand WHO is needed.
Example output:
Trades Needed
drywall
electrician
plumber
painter
flooring installer
Then:
assign subs
track bids
track COIs
store licenses

7. Inspection Checklist System
Example:
Rough Electrical Inspection
Panel labeled
Staples within code
GFCI protected
Box fill correct
Grounding verified
This turns your app into:
a field operations assistant

8. AI Job Advisor (Huge Opportunity)
This is where AI becomes useful.
User enters:
“1920s home full remodel with rewiring and 14 windows.”
AI responds:
likely permits
probable risks
recommended trades
suggested sequence
rough timeline
inspection checkpoints
That’s powerful.

Best Overall Approach
Don’t try to make the app:
“know the law perfectly.”
Instead make it:
“help contractors ask the right questions.”
That’s much more achievable and legally safer.

Smart MVP Version
If I were building V1 quickly:
I’d launch:
job intake wizard
permit trigger rules
phase generator
inspection checklist
trade recommendations
That alone is already valuable.

Long-Term Vision
You could eventually become:
contractor operations platform
compliance assistant
project workflow manager
AI estimator
permit guidance tool
Especially for:
small contractors
remodelers
handymen becoming GCs
Which is EXACTLY the market you’re currently living in yourself — meaning you understand the pain points naturally.
