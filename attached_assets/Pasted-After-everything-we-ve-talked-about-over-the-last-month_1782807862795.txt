After everything we’ve talked about over the last month, I think I’d build the service job almost like a mini case file, not just a job.

This is how I would structure it.

⸻

Service Job

1. Service Information

This identifies the work order.

* Client
* Work Order #
* Service Type
* Priority
* Warranty / Customer Pay
* Date Assigned
* Due Date

Once you select the client (Clayton, Champion, Southern, etc.), the app automatically loads that client’s billing profile and service rules.

⸻

2. Home Information

Since every service call is tied to one specific home, I’d capture:

* Homeowner Name
* Address
* Phone Number
* Builder
* Model
* Model Year
* Serial/VIN (optional)
* Dealer

If this home already exists in your system, the app simply links to it.

⸻

3. Complaint / Reported Issue

This is why you’re there.

Examples:

* Front door won’t latch.
* Kitchen faucet leaking.
* Marriage line crack.
* Vinyl siding loose.
* Floor squeak.
* Electrical outlet not working.

This should be searchable later.

⸻

4. Diagnosis

Once you arrive:

Actual Problem

For example:

* Door frame shifted.
* Loose striker plate.
* Defective faucet cartridge.

This becomes valuable historical information.

⸻

5. Repair Performed

Instead of a long paragraph, I’d use repair entries.

For example:

✔ Adjusted door hinges

✔ Shimmed jamb

✔ Replaced striker plate

✔ Tested operation

Much easier to search later.

⸻

6. Labor

Simple.

* Productive Time
* Drive Time
* Number of Technicians

No payroll here.

Just labor.

⸻

7. Parts

Every part used.

* Description
* Quantity
* Cost
* Reimbursable?

⸻

8. Photos

This is huge.

Before

During

After

Completed

If the client requires photos, the app reminds you before closing.

⸻

9. Notes

Anything unusual.

Example:

Customer requested adjustment to rear door also.

⸻

10. Billing

This is where your calculator comes in.

Since the client is already selected…

The app automatically knows:

Repair Labor

Drive Time

Mileage

Parts

Any flat rates

Then calculates the bill.

You shouldn’t have to remember rates.

The thing I would not do

I wouldn’t build the service workflow around Clayton.

I’d build it around service operations.

Clayton is simply one client.

Champion is another.

A homeowner is another.

The workflow stays the same.

Only the client’s billing profile changes.

⸻

My dream design

I actually think the service workflow should answer four questions:

1. What problem was reported?
2. What did we find?
3. What did we do to fix it?
4. How did we bill it?

Then, behind the scenes, it teaches the Service Knowledge Base:

* Which builders have the most warranty issues?
* Which models have the most recurring repairs?
* Which repairs are the most common?
* How long do those repairs usually take?
* How much do they typically cost?
* Which homes have been serviced multiple times?

That gives you something much more valuable than a collection of service tickets. It gives you a searchable history of every home you’ve touched and a growing database of repair trends that can help you make better decisions in the future. I think that fits perfectly alongside the Production Knowledge Base you’ve been designing.
