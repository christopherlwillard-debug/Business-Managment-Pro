Originally I was thinking, “Don’t let service calls teach the production Knowledge Base.”

But after thinking about it, I think they absolutely should have their own Knowledge Base.

Just not the same one.

I would split it into two knowledge systems.

Production Intelligence

This answers:

“How do we build and estimate this home?”

It learns things like:

* Average Productive Hours
* Average Crew Size
* Average Days on Site
* Gross Profit
* Material Cost
* Consumables
* Factory Defects
* Missing Materials
* Trim-out history

This is used for estimating.

⸻

Service Intelligence

This answers:

“What happens after this home is finished?”

Imagine opening a Southern Farmhouse.

Instead of only seeing production data, you also see:

Service History

Completed Service Calls: 47

Most Common Issues:

* Front door adjustment (12)
* Marriage line drywall cracks (9)
* Kitchen faucet leaks (6)
* Porch trim separating (5)
* Vinyl siding loose (4)
* Floor squeak by hallway (3)

Average Warranty Visits:

1.2 per home

Average Warranty Cost:

$185

Average Time to Complete:

1.8 hours

Now that’s incredibly valuable.

⸻

Five years later…

Dealer calls.

“Can you go back to that home?”

You pull it up.

You immediately know:

* Every visit
* Every repair
* Every photo
* Every invoice
* Every checklist
* Every part replaced

That’s huge.

⸻

This is where AI becomes useful later

Years from now your app could say:

“Southern Farmhouse homes built in 2026 have a higher-than-normal rate of front door adjustments within the first six months.”

or

“Homes from Builder X average 2.3 warranty visits while Builder Y averages 0.7.”

or

“This model has generated $18,000 in warranty labor over the last two years.”

You’d never figure that out manually.

⸻

I actually see three intelligence systems now

1. Production Intelligence

Builds estimates.

Learns labor.

Learns profitability.

⸻

2. Service Intelligence

Learns failures.

Learns warranty trends.

Learns callbacks.

Learns common repairs.

⸻

3. Customer Intelligence

Dealer history.

Homeowner history.

Repeat customers.

Average response time.

Satisfaction.

⸻

I would NOT combine them

This is important.

A warranty call should not increase the average productive hours required to trim out a home.

That would corrupt your estimating.
