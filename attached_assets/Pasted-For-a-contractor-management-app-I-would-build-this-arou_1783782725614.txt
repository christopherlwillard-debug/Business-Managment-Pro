Meal Expense Guidelines (Business Travel)

Purpose

Meal expenses can be recorded when traveling for business. The app should distinguish between:

* Local jobs (normally not deductible)
* Overnight business travel (generally deductible)
* Travel meal deduction (currently 50% deductible for most businesses under current IRS rules)

Meals should always be tax documentation expenses and should never automatically reduce a job’s profit unless the user specifically chooses to include them in job costing.

⸻

When meals are allowed

Allow meal expenses when:

* Employee or owner is traveling away from their tax home.
* Travel requires an overnight stay or enough rest that sleep is necessary.
* Meal is directly related to business travel.

Examples:

✓ Staying in a hotel while working several hours away.

✓ Traveling to another state for a project.

✓ Working several days on a manufactured home installation.

⸻

Meals that should NOT qualify

Do not allow as travel meals:

* Lunch while working locally.
* Coffee during a normal workday.
* Fast food while driving around town.
* Meals while commuting.

These should either be:

* Personal
* Non-deductible
* Company meal (if applicable)

Business Purpose examples

* Overnight construction project

⸻

IRS Documentation Reminder

Display:

“Meal expenses should include the business purpose, date, location, and receipt to support the deduction in the event of an audit.”

⸻

Tax Treatment

Current default:

Business Meals = 50% deductible

Store:

* Total Paid
* Deductible Amount
* Non-deductible Portion

Example

Total Meal: $48.00

Deductible: $24.00

Non-deductible: $24.00

This makes reporting much easier later.

Best Practice for Your App

Because your business often travels to manufactured home communities for multi-day projects, I recommend treating Meals, Hotels, Parking, and Tolls as “Travel Documentation Expenses.” They should:

* Be linked to the specific job for documentation.
* Appear in the job’s travel section.
* Flow correctly to Schedule C tax categories.
* Be excluded from production profit calculations by default.
* Be available in year-end travel and tax reports with complete audit documentation.

This keeps your production metrics accurate while preserving every legitimate business deduction and creating a clean audit trail if the IRS ever requests supporting documentation.
