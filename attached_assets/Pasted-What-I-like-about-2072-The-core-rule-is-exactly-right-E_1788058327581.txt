What I like about #2072

The core rule is exactly right:

Each dollar has one authoritative ledger and appears exactly once in the applicable reports.

That is the principle your accounting system needs.

The important pieces are:

* Owner pay → Owner Capital, never Expenses
* Subcontractors → legitimate business costs, exactly once
* Bank transactions → classification shouldn’t create duplicate accounting records
* Mileage → separate from expenses
* Tax reserves → separate from business expenses
* Owner contributions → separate from revenue
* Voids/reversals instead of deleting history
* Business and year filtering must be airtight
* Reports must all derive from consistent source records

One thing I would change

I would not let Replit automatically “repair” production financial data just because a calculation appears wrong.

Your own task actually handles this correctly:

“Do not mutate ambiguous records.”

That’s critical.

I would require the system to produce:

Before → identified source rows → proposed correction → After

and only automatically correct something when the relationship is provable.

And the subcontractor part is especially important

I agree with the distinction:

Subcontractor = expense.
Owner pay = not expense.

The mistake would be treating both merely as “payments.”

A subcontractor payment affects the business’s profitability. An owner withdrawal is a distribution of business equity/capital.

So the final accounting model should essentially be:

Revenue
− Operating expenses
− Subcontractor costs
= Business profit

while:

Owner withdrawals
→ Owner Capital / Equity

and do not reduce operating profit.
