Here’s how to think about it:

🔗 How the setup actually works
You’re not really “linking your bank to two apps” in the same way—you’re giving different types of access:
1. Your Replit app (expense tracking side)

Plaid
Finicity

Read transactions only
Pull balances, expenses, deposits
DO NOT move money

Gusto connects directly to your bank via ACH to:
Withdraw payroll funds
Pay employees
Handle tax withholdings and filings
👉 This is write access (money movement), totally separate from Plaid-type tools.

These systems do NOT sync with each other automatically.
So:
Gusto pulls money → shows payroll in Gusto
Your app (via Plaid) sees the transaction → logs it as an expense

Gusto doesn’t “feed” your app payroll data unless you integrate it manually.

Create a backend function called optimizeRoute(jobs) in Node.js.

Each job has:
- sqft
- revenue
- estimated_time_minutes

Use constants:
- MAX_SQFT_PER_DAY = 12000
- MAX_WORK_MINUTES = 480
- MIN_REVENUE_PER_JOB = 250
- LABOR_COST_PER_HOUR = 60
- MATERIAL_COST_PER_SQFT = 0.08

Steps:
1. Filter out jobs where revenue < MIN_REVENUE_PER_JOB
2. For each job:
   - labor_cost = (estimated_time_minutes / 60) * LABOR_COST_PER_HOUR
   - material_cost = sqft * MATERIAL_COST_PER_SQFT
   - profit = revenue - labor_cost - material_cost
   - profit_per_minute = profit / estimated_time_minutes
3. Remove jobs with profit <= 0
4. Sort jobs by profit_per_minute descending
5. Loop through jobs and add them until:
   - total_sqft exceeds MAX_SQFT_PER_DAY
   - total_minutes exceeds MAX_WORK_MINUTES
6. Return:
   - selected_jobs
   - total_sqft
   - total_revenue
   - total_profit
   - total_minutes

Return:
- recommended_price
