Right now your task hardcodes the rules for a single-member LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship. That works for you today, but your app is clearly evolving into software other contractors could use. If someone is an S corp or partnership, the accounting treatment changes.

I'd build a Business Tax Profile that drives the accounting rules instead of sprinkling tax-type logic throughout the code.

For example:

Business Tax Profile

Under Business Settings → Tax Profile

Entity Type
Sole Proprietorship
Single-Member LLC (Disregarded Entity)
Multi-Member LLC (Partnership)
S Corporation
C Corporation
Nonprofit (future)
Tax Treatment
Sole Proprietorship
Partnership
S Corporation
C Corporation
Accounting Engine

Instead of hardcoding:

if category == "Owner Draw"

The shared computeNetProfit() function would use the business's tax profile.

Example:

switch (business.taxTreatment)

Sole Proprietorship
    Owner Draws excluded
    Owner Contributions excluded

Partnership
    Member Draws excluded
    Member Contributions excluded

S Corporation
    Owner Distributions excluded
    Officer Payroll included as expense

C Corporation
    Dividends excluded
    Officer Payroll included as expense

Now the accounting engine automatically behaves correctly based on the business configuration.

UI

The informational card could even adapt automatically.

For your business today:

Owner Activity

Owner Contributions

Owner Draws

Owner draws are distributions of owner equity.
They do not affect business profit.

If the business later elects S Corporation:

Shareholder Activity

Capital Contributions

Distributions

Shareholder distributions do not affect Profit & Loss.
Officer wages are reported separately as payroll expense.

No code changes—just different business settings.

Future-proofing

I'd even add this as a new section to your task:

Business Tax Profile

All accounting rules related to owner equity, draws, distributions, and payroll must be driven by the Business Tax Profile rather than hardcoded.

The Business Tax Profile determines:

Whether Owner Draws affect accounting (they generally should not).
Whether Officer Payroll is deductible.
Which informational labels are displayed (Owner Activity, Member Activity, Shareholder Activity, etc.).
Future tax calculations and reporting behavior.

The default profile for newly created businesses should be:

Entity Type: Single-Member LLC
Tax Treatment: Sole Proprietorship

I think this is one of those architectural decisions that will pay off. Right now it's just you, but you've already built support for multiple businesses. If someone creates an S corporation in your app a year from now, you won't have to rewrite the accounting engine—you'll just add the appropriate rules to the tax profile. That separation between business configuration and accounting logic is a much more scalable foundation.
