Architectural Notes for Task #1040

After thinking through the Knowledge Base design, I want to establish a few architectural rules before implementation. These aren’t intended to change the core functionality of Task #1040, but rather to define how the Knowledge Base should evolve as the application grows.

1. Knowledge Records are the Source of Truth

Once a job has gone through the Teach the Knowledge Base workflow, the resulting Knowledge Record should become the official historical record for that completed job.

The Knowledge Base should not continuously recalculate historical metrics from live operational data whenever possible. Instead, reports, averages, benchmarks, forecasting, and business intelligence should use the approved Knowledge Records.

The workflow should be:

Create Job

↓

Complete Work

↓

Financials

↓

Labor Summary

↓

Teach the Knowledge Base

↓

Knowledge Record Created

↓

Historical Analytics Updated

This creates a reviewed and approved historical record instead of relying on operational data that may continue changing after the job is completed.

⸻

2. Knowledge Record Status

Instead of only using a simple taught = true/false flag, I would like the Knowledge Record to support a status field so the system can grow without redesigning the database later.

Suggested statuses:

* Draft
    * Automatically generated from current job data.
    * Not yet reviewed.
* Reviewed
    * Chris has reviewed and possibly edited the values but has not committed them to the Knowledge Base.
* Accepted
    * Officially taught to the Knowledge Base.
    * Included in historical averages.
* Excluded
    * Stored for historical reference and auditing but intentionally excluded from Knowledge Base averages.

This provides much more flexibility than a simple boolean while preserving the current workflow.

⸻

3. Snapshot Builder and Model Information

Knowledge Records should preserve what existed at the time the job was completed.

Along with storing foreign keys, each Knowledge Record should also snapshot the builder and model names.

Example:

* builder_id
* builder_name_snapshot
* model_id
* model_name_snapshot

If a builder later changes their name or a model is renamed, the historical Knowledge Record should continue reflecting what the job was originally completed under.

Historical records should never change simply because today’s master data changed.

⸻

4. Historical Reports Should Read Knowledge Records

Whenever practical, reporting features should read from approved Knowledge Records rather than reconstructing values from live job data.

Examples include:

* Average Productive Hours
* Average Travel Hours
* Average Crew Size
* Average Days on Site
* Average Invoice
* Average Material Cost
* Average Consumables
* Average Gross Profit
* Average Net Profit
* Historical Performance reports
* Future estimating intelligence

If a mistake is discovered later, the correction should be made to the Knowledge Record through the Learning History editor. The version history will preserve the original values, edit reason, and timestamps.

This creates an auditable history instead of silently changing past results.

⸻

5. Confidence Indicator

Every Builder/Model profile should display a confidence indicator based on how many accepted Knowledge Records contributed to the averages.

Examples:

* Based on 2 accepted jobs
* Based on 18 accepted jobs
* Based on 74 accepted jobs

The goal is to communicate how reliable the averages are.

⸻

6. Long-Term Vision

The Knowledge Base is becoming much more than a collection of averages.

It is evolving into the company’s historical intelligence engine.

Every completed job should improve future estimates by teaching the application:

* How much productive labor different builders and models require.
* Typical travel requirements.
* Average crew size.
* Average job duration.
* Average financial performance.
* Common missing materials.
* Common factory defects.
* Warranty frequency.
* Historical profitability.
* Future estimating benchmarks.

The long-term objective is for every completed job to make the application smarter while keeping every learned value transparent, reviewable, editable, versioned, and traceable back to the job that created it.

The application should never behave like a black box. Every average should be explainable, every learned value should be inspectable, and Chris should always retain final authority over what becomes part of the company’s historical knowledge.
