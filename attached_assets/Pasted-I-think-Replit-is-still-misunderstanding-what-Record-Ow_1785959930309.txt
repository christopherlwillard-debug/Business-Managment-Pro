I think Replit is still misunderstanding what Record Owner Pay is supposed to be.

Based on everything we've discussed, I would not make it just a status marker.

Here's why:

If you pay yourself in three separate transfers, a simple "Paid" status is wrong.

Example:

My Share
$1,299.33

Transfer #1
$500

Transfer #2
$300

Transfer #3
$499.33

A single "Paid" flag can't represent that.

What I would build instead
Owner Withdrawals

Transfer #1
$500.00
Aug 5

Transfer #2
$300.00
Aug 8

Transfer #3
$499.33
Aug 12

--------------------

Total Withdrawn
$1,299.33

Remaining Available
$0.00

Notice something important...

This is NOT an expense.

It's also NOT just a status.

It's a withdrawal ledger.

I'd also remove this from the P&L

I don't like:

Owner Activity

Remaining Owner Equity

That belongs on an equity report or owner dashboard, not on a job P&L.

The P&L should stop at:

Revenue

Business Expenses

Growth

Hourly Workers

Net Business Profit

Everything after that belongs in Pay Breakdown, because that's where you're allocating the profit.

A job may have multiple owner withdrawals over time.

Owner withdrawals belong in the Pay Breakdown workflow, not on the Profit & Loss statement.

The Pay Breakdown page should answer one question:

"How much of my share have I already withdrawn, and how much is still available to withdraw?"
One more thought

I actually think the button shouldn't even be called "Record Owner Pay."

I'd rename it to:

Record Owner Withdrawal
Withdraw Profit
Transfer to Personal
Record Owner Draw

"Owner Pay" sounds like payroll, and that's exactly what's causing the confusion. A sole proprietor isn't paying themselves wages—they're withdrawing money they already own. Using "Owner Withdrawal" or "Owner Draw" makes the intent much clearer and aligns with the workflow you're building.

Those are answering two different questions:

Take Home: "How much of my share is actually mine after setting taxes aside?"
Remaining Available to Withdraw: "How much of that share have I not transferred to myself yet?"

That's the behavior I think you're after.

The calculation of your split and your estimated take-home after taxes should never change because you recorded a withdrawal. Recording a withdrawal is just tracking that you've already moved some of that money from the business account to your personal account.
