I agree. The current wording is wrong for your business structure today.

You're not doing a "profit share" in the legal/accounting sense. You're a single-member LLC and you're simply allocating part of a job's profit to a subcontractor based on your agreement for that specific job.

I'd rewrite that Replit task more like this:

Job Payment Tracking & Auto-Expense Flow (Revised)
Important Business Logic

This is NOT a profit-sharing system.

Willard's General Contracting is currently a single-member LLC.

The 50/50 split shown in the Job Income Summary is simply a job-level earnings allocation between the owner and a subcontractor based on an agreed percentage of the job profit after labor and growth reserve deductions.

The subcontractor payment should be treated as a subcontractor expense, not a profit distribution or equity share.

Payment Types
Hourly Workers
Paid hours × rate
Deducted before job profit calculations
Recorded as Labor Expense
Percentage-Based Subcontractors
Paid a percentage of the "To Split" amount
Example: 50% of remaining profit after labor and growth reserve
Recorded as Subcontractor Expense
NOT labeled "Profit Share"
Owner Allocation
Remaining amount allocated to owner
Used for job profitability reporting
Can optionally be recorded as an Owner Draw
NOT required to close the job
Job Income Flow

Job Revenue

minus

Hourly Worker Costs

equals

Remaining

minus

Growth Reserve (5%)

equals

To Split

then

Percentage-Based Subcontractor Allocation

and

Owner Allocation

are calculated from To Split.

Payment Tracking
Hourly Workers

Each hourly worker should have:

Amount Owed
Status (Unpaid / Paid)
Paid Date
Mark Paid button

When marked paid:

Create labor expense
Link expense to job
Update accounting automatically
Percentage-Based Subcontractors

Each subcontractor should have:

Allocated Amount
Status (Unpaid / Paid)
Paid Date
Mark Paid button

When marked paid:

Create subcontractor expense
Link expense to job
Update accounting automatically
Owner Allocation

Show owner allocation for reporting purposes.

Optional button:

"Record Owner Draw"

When clicked:

Create Owner Draw transaction
Do not classify as labor expense
Do not classify as subcontractor expense
Job Close Requirements

A job can be closed when:

Customer invoice is paid
All hourly workers are marked paid
All subcontractor allocations are marked paid

Owner draw is optional and should not prevent job closure.

Accounting Integration

The user should never manually create duplicate expense entries.

When any worker or subcontractor is marked paid:

Expense record is automatically created
Expense is linked to the job
Accounting dashboard updates
Job profitability updates
Expense reports update

The Job Financials screen should be the single source of truth for all job-related payments.

This keeps everything aligned with how you're actually operating right now. Later, if you elect S-Corp status and start taking payroll or true profit distributions, you can add a separate profit-sharing or owner compensation system. For now, "Percentage-Based Subcontractor" or "Job Allocation" is the correct terminology.
