I actually agree with you. After thinking through how your entire app is structured, I would keep the word "Client."

The important distinction isn't changing the terminology—it's understanding that different client types use different pricing systems.

For example:

Clients

├── Clayton Homes - Owensboro
│      Type: MH Production Dealer
│
├── Clayton Homes - Evansville
│      Type: MH Production Dealer
│
├── Cavco Home Center
│      Type: MH Production Dealer
│
├── John Smith
│      Type: Residential Customer
│
├── ABC Property Management
│      Type: Commercial Customer
│
└── Future Dealer
       Type: MH Production Dealer

Every one of those is still a client.

The difference is that each client belongs to a client type, and that determines how pricing works.

MH Production Dealer → Uses the Production Pricing Engine.
Residential Customer → Uses your Contracting estimate.
Service Customer → Uses your Service pricing calculator.

So instead of renaming everything to "Dealer Pricing," I'd simply describe it as:

Client Production Pricing

or

Production Pricing by Client

That way, the pricing engine still asks:

Who is the client?

If the client is Clayton Homes – Owensboro, it uses Clayton's production pricing.

If the client is Cavco, it uses Cavco's production pricing.

If you add ABC Manufactured Housing next year, you simply create a new client and assign its production pricing.

I would update the spec like this:
Layer 2 – Client Production Pricing

Each client can have its own production pricing schedule.

For Manufactured Home Production jobs, these clients are typically manufactured home dealers, such as:

Clayton Homes – Owensboro
Clayton Homes – Evansville
Cavco Home Center
Future production dealers

Only prices that differ from the Master Production Billing Catalog need to be stored. Any production item without a client-specific override automatically falls back to the master price.

This keeps the terminology consistent throughout the entire application, where everyone you invoice is a client, while still allowing Production, Service, and Contracting to use different pricing methods based on the type of work being performed.

I think that's the cleanest approach because it aligns with how you've already designed the rest of the app. The Clients table remains the single source of truth for everyone you do business with, and each module (Production, Service, or Contracting) simply uses the pricing logic appropriate for that type of job.
