H3 — Mileage counts

The audit found that the mileage dollars and mileage-count/warning logic don’t use identical predicates.

That needs to be unified so the app can’t say something like:

5,505 deductible miles
228 mileage records

when those numbers are coming from different populations.

M1/M2 — Job profitability

Right now:

Job Profit: based on invoiced revenue
Business Accounting: based on cash revenue

That’s not necessarily wrong, but the app needs to clearly distinguish:

Job profitability based on earned/invoiced revenue

from:

Business cash-basis profitability

And ideally both should use a common profit engine where appropriate.

⸻

M3/M4 — Mileage accounting model

The app has two different mileage systems:

* individual/manual mileage → individual expense
* MileIQ/imported mileage → aggregate expense

That’s okay if intentional, but it needs one reconciliation layer so imports, edits, personal/business changes, and rate changes don’t create inconsistencies.

This is especially important given your current mileage problem.

🟡 Tier 3 — CRM/customer system

This is a big one for where you’re taking Willard’s.

The audit confirmed your current CRM doesn’t properly model:

Company → Multiple Contacts → Multiple Locations → Multiple Opportunities → Jobs → Estimates → Invoices

That’s exactly what you were talking about earlier with:

“A company and multiple contacts.”

I’d fix this before the CRM grows much more.

The proper structure should be something like:

Organization

* Willard’s General Contracting
    * Contact: John
    * Contact: Sarah
    * Contact: Purchasing
    * Location: Evansville
    * Location: Owensboro
    * Location: Job Site A
    * Opportunity: Home #123
    * Opportunity: Home #124

Then jobs/invoices/estimates attach to the appropriate organization/contact/site.

This also fixes H12 and helps with H13.

The biggest thing I’d tell Replit

Don’t let it turn this into 30 unrelated little tasks.

The audit is telling us there are several foundations that need to be established first.

The biggest foundation is:

Business → Source Record → Accounting Treatment → Report

Every financial number should be traceable backward through that chain.

Once that’s true, things like your $45,106.54 Expenses, $32,515.47 Accounting, James’s allocation payments, mileage, Schedule C, and cash-basis revenue become independently verifiable instead of us having to trust whatever number happens to appear on a screen.

And I would not let Replit make any production data changes for these audit items until the read-only reconciliation is finished and approved.
