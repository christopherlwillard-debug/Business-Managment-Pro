I think this is a solid feature request overall. The only thing I'd change is the naming of the database field.

excludeFromJobProfit works, but it's a negative flag, and those tend to become confusing over time.

I'd instead use:

countsTowardJobProfit (boolean, default true)

Then your code becomes much easier to read:

if (expense.countsTowardJobProfit) {
    jobExpenseTotal += expense.amount;
}

instead of

if (!expense.excludeFromJobProfit) {
    jobExpenseTotal += expense.amount;
}

The UI can still say "Count toward job profit?" exactly as you've written it.

I think you're looking at this exactly the right way.

This feature isn't really about job costing—it's about creating an audit trail.

If the IRS or your CPA ever asks, "Why did you stay at a Hampton Inn in Indiana?" you can open the job and immediately show:

Job: Quincy, IN – Clayton Warranty Work
Job Dates: July 10–12
Job Address: 123 Main St., Quincy, IN
Customer: Clayton Homes
Photos: Before/after
Daily Logs: Crew worked July 10–12
Invoice: $3,200
Hotel: Hampton Inn – $189
Meals: Texas Roadhouse – $42
Parking: $15

Everything tells one complete story.

That's much stronger documentation than having hotel receipts sitting in QuickBooks with no context.

I'd even simplify the feature request.

Instead of thinking of it as "documentation-only expenses," think of it as:

Link this expense to a job as supporting documentation.

The link exists so you can prove why the expense occurred, not because it's part of the job's profitability.

The expense still:

✅ Shows on your business P&L.
✅ Is deductible (subject to the applicable tax rules).
✅ Appears in banking and expense reports.

It simply doesn't affect the job's operational profit.

For your business, especially since you're traveling to manufactured home jobs in different states, this is exactly the kind of documentation that makes tax time much easier. Every hotel, meal, and travel expense has a corresponding job, invoice, address, photos, and work history to support it. That's a very clean record if anyone ever asks why those travel expenses were incurred.
