What "Liability" Means

In accounting terms:

Revenue collected = money you received.
Subcontractor not paid = money you owe someone.

That unpaid amount is a liability.

Example:

Bank account:

Customer paid you $3,000

But:

Dewayne still owed $950

You don't really have all $3,000 available because $950 belongs to someone else.

So the system is saying:

"Hey, this job still has unpaid obligations."

One Change I'd Make

Since you're not doing profit sharing, I'd have Replit change the wording from:

Outstanding Sub Liability

to something like:

Outstanding Worker Payments

or

Unpaid Job Payments

because "liability" is accountant language and not very user-friendly.

For example:

🟧 Unpaid Job Payments

Dewayne: $950
Worker 2: $500

Total Due: $1,450

[Go to Payments]

That's probably easier to understand when you're quickly scrolling through jobs.

What & Why
Fixed and allocation subs only appear in expenses after "Mark Paid" is clicked on the job card. If a job is closed/completed without the owner clicking Mark Paid, those costs never enter the books — the outstanding liability warning added in the parent task helps visibility but doesn't fix the root cause. Auto-creating the expense (or at least prompting the user) when a job is completed would close the gap entirely.

I would not choose option (a) where it automatically marks them paid.

There's a huge accounting difference between:

Owing someone money
Actually paying them

Example:

You complete a $10,000 job today.

Dewayne is owed $2,500.

You don't pay him until Friday.

For those 3 days:

Expense should exist ✔️
Liability should exist ✔️
Payment should NOT be marked paid ❌

If the system auto-marks him paid, your books will show money leaving the company that never actually left.

What I would do instead

When you click Complete Job, show:

Unpaid Worker Alert

This job has unpaid worker allocations:

Dewayne: $950
Worker 2: $500

Total Due: $1,450

What would you like to do?

[Mark All Paid Now]

[Complete Job & Keep As Outstanding]

If You Choose "Mark All Paid Now"

Then:

Creates expense entries
Marks workers paid
Records payment date
Removes outstanding balance
If You Choose "Complete Job & Keep As Outstanding"

Then:

Creates the expense entries
Creates an Accounts Payable / Outstanding Worker Payment record
Keeps payment status as Unpaid
Shows the warning banner
Keeps amount in outstanding liabilities

That is much closer to real-world bookkeeping.

For Your App

I'd tell Replit:

Do not automatically mark workers paid when a job is completed. Completing a job and paying workers are two separate events.

When a job is completed:

Create the subcontractor labor expense if it does not already exist.
