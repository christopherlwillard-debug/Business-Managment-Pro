#1735 - Estimated Fuel Cost for Operational Profit

What & Why

We use the IRS Standard Mileage Deduction for tax purposes, so fuel receipts are not required for accounting or taxes. However, we still need to understand the true profitability of each job.

This feature adds an "Estimated Fuel Cost" to the Job Financials P&L tab. The value is informational only and is used to calculate a more realistic Operational Profit. It does not create an expense, affect accounting reports, or impact tax calculations.

Instead of estimating a single round trip, the estimate should account for every day the crew traveled to and from the job site.

Done Looks Like

- Add an "Estimated Fuel Cost" line to the Job Financials → P&L tab.
- Fuel cost is calculated automatically.
- Estimated Fuel Cost is NOT an accounting expense.
- Estimated Fuel Cost is NOT included in Job Expenses.
- Estimated Fuel Cost is NOT used for tax reporting.
- Operational Profit = Revenue - Job Expenses - Estimated Fuel Cost.

Fuel Cost Calculation

The estimate should use:

- Business Address (already stored)
- Job Address (already stored)
- Default Vehicle MPG (Business Settings)
- Current Kentucky average fuel price (API)
- Number of actual workdays on the job

Formula

One-Way Route Distance
× 2 (Round Trip)
× Number of Workdays
= Total Estimated Miles

Total Estimated Miles
÷ Vehicle MPG
× Current Fuel Price
= Estimated Fuel Cost

Workday Calculation

Do NOT use calendar days.

Instead, use the number of days the crew actually worked.

Priority:

1. Count Daily Logs linked to the job.
2. If Daily Logs are unavailable, fall back to Job Start Date → Completion Date.
