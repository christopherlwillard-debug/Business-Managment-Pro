I actually think this is the right scope.

More importantly, I think it avoids the mistake that happened before—trying to solve five future problems before solving today's problem.

There are only three small changes I'd make.

1. Remove "gets smarter every time a job is completed"

I'd simplify the opening.

Instead of:

Build a simple knowledge base for manufactured homes that gets smarter every time a job is completed.

I'd say:

Build a simple Manufactured Home Knowledge Base that stores one permanent record for each manufactured home model and uses completed jobs to provide historical information for estimating future work.

It's less marketing language and more of a product requirement.

2. Don't mention "analytics engine"

This sentence:

The goal is a tool that makes the next MFH job easier to estimate, with no analytics engine or complex features yet.

I'd replace with:

The goal of Phase 1 is to establish a clean data model and a practical knowledge base. Advanced analytics, benchmarking, forecasting, and other intelligence features are intentionally out of scope for this phase.

That keeps the focus on what is being built instead of what isn't.

3. Make the "derived statistics" requirement more general

Instead of:

Statistics are derived from completed jobs at query time; no duplicate data is stored.

I'd write:

Statistics must be derived from completed jobs rather than maintained as duplicate data. The implementation should prioritize correctness and maintainability.

That gives Replit flexibility. If they later decide to use a cached view or another optimization, they're still meeting the requirement.
