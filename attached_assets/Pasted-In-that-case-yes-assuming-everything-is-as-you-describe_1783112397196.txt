I actually think this is an opportunity to make your app better for both operations and taxes.

Right now, it sounds like your "Job Expenses" are really job costing. Those expenses reduce the job's profitability.

But not every deductible business expense should reduce the profit of a specific job.

I'd separate them into two concepts:

Type	Affects Job Profit?	Tax Deductible?	Examples
Job Cost	✅ Yes	✅ Yes	Materials, subcontractors, permits, dumpster, equipment rental
Travel & Business Expense	Optional	✅ Yes	Hotel, meals, mileage, parking, tolls

⬜ No (default for lodging, meals, owner travel, etc.)

Example:

Job

Revenue: $6,000
Materials: -$1,200
Labor: -$1,800
Job Profit: $3,000

Now you stay in a hotel for $180.

Instead of reducing the job profit to $2,820, it would:

Be linked to that job for documentation.
Show under a Travel tab on the job.
Count toward your business tax deductions.
Still appear in reports like "Travel Expenses by Job" or "Travel Expenses by Client."
Not distort the operational profitability of the job.
Why this matters

You're trying to answer two different questions:

How profitable was this job?
How much did my business spend that is tax deductible?

Those answers aren't always the same.

For example, if you spend:

$220 on a hotel,
$45 on meals,
$35 on fuel,

those are legitimate business expenses, but you may not want them making it look like the crew did a less profitable job. Instead, you want to know, "This out-of-town job required $300 of travel expenses."

I think this fits your app well

Since you're building a contractor management system, I'd actually have two expense sections on every job:

Job Costs (used to calculate job profitability)
Linked Business Expenses (for documentation, tax reporting, and expense tracking)

This gives you the best of both worlds:

Accurate job costing and labor analytics.
Complete tax documentation.
The ability to report things like, "We spent $8,450 on lodging this year," or "Clayton jobs required $2,100 in travel expenses."

I think this approach is much more scalable as your company grows and starts taking more out-of-town manufactured home projects.
