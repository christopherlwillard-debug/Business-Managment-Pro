This is in excellent shape. I only have two changes I’d recommend before sending it to Replit.

1. Don’t use a calculated “Audit Readiness Score”

I would replace the 9/10 score with an Audit Checklist and an overall status.

The reason is that a score is subjective. For example, is a missing receipt worth the same as a missing business purpose? The IRS doesn’t use a point system—they care whether required documentation exists.

Instead, show something like:

Audit Status

* 🟢 Audit Ready
* 🟡 Needs Attention

Then list the checklist:

* ✓ Business Purpose
* ✓ Destination
* ✓ Travel Dates
* ✓ Receipt Photos (or explanation for missing receipts)
* ✓ Meal Overnight Status
* ✓ Job Linked (optional if applicable)

If something is missing:

* ⚠ Missing receipt explanation
* ⚠ Meal missing overnight-travel answer

This is simpler, easier to understand, and less arbitrary.

⸻

2. Keep Business Purpose at the Trip Level

You currently collect Business Purpose on both the trip and the receipt.

I’d treat them differently:

Trip Business Purpose

Overall reason for the trip.

Example:

Warranty repairs for Clayton Homes – Evansville Community.

Receipt Business Purpose
Default to the trip’s business purpose, but allow editing if needed.

For example:

Trip:

Warranty repairs for Clayton Homes – Evansville Community.

Hotel:

(inherits trip purpose)

Meal:

Dinner while performing warranty repairs.

This saves data entry while still allowing more detailed documentation when appropriate.

⸻

One small enhancement

I’d add these two fields to the business_trips table:

* closed_at
* closed_by

When a trip is marked Completed, record when and by whom it was closed. It’s a small addition but creates a stronger audit trail and is useful if you ever have multiple users in the app.

⸻

Overall

I’d consider this feature essentially complete. It fits your workflow well:

* No duplicate mileage tracking.
* Good separation between job costing and tax documentation.
* Proper handling of meal deductions.
* Logical organization of receipts by trip.
* Clean integration with jobs and reports.
* Scalable if you add employees or office staff later.

The only thing I’d remove is the numeric “Audit Readiness” score in favor of a checklist with an overall status, since that’s more objective and easier to maintain over time.
